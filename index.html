Tab Daddy — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 15 October 2026

Tab Daddy groups your tabs on your own computer. It does not sell data, show ads, or use analytics. The only thing that can ever leave your computer is a short list of words used to name groups, and only if you turn on AI naming.

What the extension reads, on your computer

  • Tab titles and addresses, through Chrome's tabs API, to find duplicate tabs, to recognise well-known sites and web apps (for example, a Google Sheet goes in Sheets, Amazon in Shopping) using a list built into the extension, and to group tabs it can't read.
  • A short summary of each open page (its title, description, main headings and the first 1,500 characters of its main text), but only after you click Allow to give Tab Daddy access to the pages you have open. This is how tabs are grouped by what they're about.

Page summaries are held in memory while the popup is open and discarded when it closes. They are never written to disk and never sent anywhere.

What is stored on your computer

Using Chrome's storage API, Tab Daddy keeps: whether you turned AI naming on or off; a random install ID (see below); and recent group names it received, keyed by the words they were made from, for up to 7 days, so reopening the popup on the same tabs sends nothing. You can clear all of this by removing the extension.

AI naming (optional, off until you say yes)

The first time there are groups to name, Tab Daddy asks whether to name them with AI. If you say yes, then for each new group it sends:

  • up to eight ordinary words that appear on at least two of the group's tabs, for example mortgage, rates, loan, fixed. A word written with a capital letter on any of those tabs, such as a person's or a place's name, is never sent;
  • a random install ID, so the server can stop any one install from using more than its share.

Only groups of related pages are named this way; groups named for what a site is used for (Shopping, Coding, News…) are named on your computer and send nothing. Page menus, headers, sidebars and hidden text are never read. Words containing numbers or symbols are removed, site names are removed, and pages with a password, payment-card or one-time-code field contribute nothing. Page text, page titles and web addresses are never sent.

The words go to Tab Daddy's naming server, which runs on Cloudflare. The server passes the words, without the install ID, to Groq, which runs an open AI model that suggests a short name. The server keeps each word list and its suggested name for up to 7 days so that the same words can be named again without asking the model; this cache is shared by all users and contains no install IDs. The server does not keep logs of requests. Cloudflare necessarily sees your IP address in order to deliver each request; Tab Daddy does not record it.

You can turn AI naming off at any time from the line under the groups list. With it off, nothing is sent and groups are named on your computer.

Third parties

Cloudflare (hosting the naming server) and Groq (suggesting names) receive the data described above, only when AI naming is on, and only to produce group names. No other third parties. No advertising networks, no analytics, no remote code. Data is never sold or used for anything other than naming your tab groups.

Permissions

  • tabs: read tab titles and addresses to group tabs and find duplicates.
  • tabGroups: create Chrome tab groups and add tabs to groups you already have.
  • scripting: run the small page-summary reader in your open tabs.
  • storage: remember your AI-naming choice and recent group names.
  • Access to the sites you have open (optional, asked for when you click Allow): lets the page-summary reader run.
